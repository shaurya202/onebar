    What OneBar does

    OneBar routes you to shelter when the network is down. Download a map for your area once, and the routing runs on this phone — with no signal, in airplane mode, in a blackout.

    It is a map, not a rescue service. It shows hazards, shows shelters, and finds a way between them. It cannot see you, cannot dispatch anyone, and does not know you are in trouble.
    This is not a replacement for emergency services. In an emergency, call your local emergency number first. OneBar cannot contact anyone on your behalf.
    Shelters come from OpenStreetMap. They are real, mapped places, but OneBar cannot confirm a building is open or staffed right now. Treat every shelter as unconfirmed unless an authority says otherwise.
    Hazards come from NWS, USGS and NASA. When there are no alerts for your area, OneBar tells you so. It never invents hazards to fill the map.

    OneBar needs your location to route you out of where you actually are.

    It stays on this device. Your position is used to draw the map, pick a shelter and compute a route. It is only ever sent anywhere if you choose to put it in an SOS message.

    Read the full privacy statement — what is stored on this phone, what a shared report uploads, and what never leaves the device.

    Who should hear from you if you need help? One tap fills them into an SOS message with your position and where you are heading.

    Stored on this phone only. OneBar has no account to attach them to and never uploads them. The message is sent by your own texting app, which is why it still works with no data.
